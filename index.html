First author · 2026

Mapping the Genuine Advantage of Quantum AI in Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

With F. J. Afseen
The question

Drug companies are being urged to fund quantum computing for tasks like inspecting tablets on a production line and screening candidate molecules. These are expensive, long-term bets, and the claim behind them had not been tested under controlled conditions.

The finding

Across seven public datasets — one identical process, changing only the data — quantum models did no better than ordinary ones. I showed that two of the three standard ways of encoding data into a quantum circuit are ordinary mathematics in disguise, so they cannot win in principle, and traced the remaining gap on images to how the data was compressed rather than to the quantum model. The paper concludes that the nearer-term value of quantum computing lies in simulating molecules, not classifying data, and gives a short set of checks a company can run before funding a pilot.

Co-author · 2026

What Changes and What Survives Across Days in Surface EMG Decoding

With M. Kabir
The question

Surface electromyography reads muscle activity through electrodes on the skin and drives prosthetic hands, rehabilitation devices and wearable controllers. Accuracy falls sharply the next time the device is worn, which is one reason people stop using them. Across 63 participants in two public datasets we tested the two intuitive fixes: place electrodes on each person's strongest muscle site, and correct for electrode movement afterwards.

The finding

The fix is rescaling the signal, not repositioning the electrodes. Concentrating electrodes on the strongest site raises the signal 1.7-fold within one session, but across days it loses to spreading them out, and adding more electrodes does not buy the loss back. No correction for electrode movement beat simply doing nothing. What works is rescaling each channel from a single unlabelled calibration contraction — recovering 44.5% of the accuracy lost between days and matching supervised retraining, without asking the user for new labels.

AI evaluation

NoteFidelity

Independent project · ongoing · open source
The problem

Hospitals are starting to use AI that listens to a consultation and writes the clinical note. Scoring these tools by comparing wording against a reference note means a note can look good while omitting something that matters — a medication dose, say.

What I built

An open evaluation suite that checks whether the facts spoken in the consultation actually reached the note, and grades each missing fact by how much harm it could cause. I built four ways of checking a fact against a note and measured them against clinician judgement. I set the required level of agreement before starting, did not reach it, and published that result rather than lowering the standard. Work continues.

Machine learning

Skin Cancer Detection from Dermatology Images

University team project · team of three
The problem

Clinical records already carry a lot of signal — age, lesion site, patient history. Before claiming an imaging model helps, you have to measure what the photograph adds on top of what the notes already say.

What we built

A controlled comparison of six machine learning models on 10,015 skin lesion photographs. The best image model reached 87% accuracy against 83% for models given only patient details. We split the data by lesion rather than by photograph — several images of one lesion appearing in both training and testing would have flattered the results.

Civic tech · Live

Chokh-e-Dekha

Individual project · live
The problem

In Bangladesh, reports of damaged roads, uncollected rubbish and utility failures disappear into government offices with no way to follow up.

What I built

Residents report a problem with a photo and location, then track it publicly until it is resolved. It groups duplicate reports, routes each one to the department responsible, marks a problem fixed only when the original reporter confirms it, and drafts formal complaint letters in English and Bengali when an authority does not respond.

Pharmazine

Started at Xactidea Ltd. · later extended independently
The problem

What I built

A pharmacy management and point-of-sale system handling sales, stock and purchasing across branches down to individual batches. It reorders automatically when stock runs low or nears expiry, and separates permissions for owners, managers and staff with two-step sign-in.
